Directory Traversal Hex

Incantation

find /var/log -type f -name "*.log" -mtime +7 -size +1M -exec ls -lh {} \;

Invocation

# Cast on your own folder (no sudo needed)
find ~/projects -type f -name "*.log" -mtime +7 -size +1M -exec ls -lh {} \;

# Sample effect
-rw-r--r-- 1 sorcerer sorcerer 2.0M Oct 27 15:52 /home/sorcerer/projects/app/old.log
-rw-r--r-- 1 sorcerer sorcerer 4.3M Oct 20 09:11 /home/sorcerer/projects/api/logs/old2.log

Effect

In plain terms: Searches a folder and all its subfolders for .log files that are older than 7 days and bigger than 1 MB, then prints their size and date.

The hex walks every corridor of the directory tree and marks the stale scrolls that weigh down the archive. It only points at them. It does not burn them.

find /var/log starts the walk at that folder and goes down every level. -type f keeps only regular files, -name "*.log" matches the extension (the quotes stop the shell from expanding the * too early), -mtime +7 means last modified more than 7 days ago, and -size +1M means larger than 1 MB. -exec ls -lh {} \; runs ls -lh on each match, where {} is the file path.

Side Effects

  • Reading /var/log may print Permission denied for some files without root. Add 2>/dev/null to hide those lines, or point the hex at your own folder.
  • Swapping -exec ls -lh {} \; for -delete turns this SAFE spell into a FORBIDDEN one that permanently removes the files. Run the read-only version first.
